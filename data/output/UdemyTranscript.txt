

======================================================================
Lecture-1
======================================================================

Welcome to Star Group. Imagine this is your first week here as a junior developer. You are still

getting familiar with the company, the team and the systems people use every day. Star Group

operates different kinds of vehicles, private cars, micro buses, ambulances and delivery vans.

But much of the information about those vehicles is still maintained manually in spreadsheets.

Adding a new vehicle, checking an existing record or correcting information

means going back to those files again and again. It works, but it is hardly convenient.

One morning, the head of administration comes to the IT team with a straightforward request.

We need a simple way to manage our vehicle records. They are not asking for a huge enterprise

platform. They want a small application that solves the immediate need and works properly.

The senior developers are already focused on larger projects, so your team lead turns to you

and says, keep it simple, follow the requirement, use AI and build a working version.


======================================================================
Lecture-2
======================================================================

Here is what you will build by the end of this course.

A small, working vehicle management application for Stark Group.

The scope is intentionally focused.

An authorized user will log in, register a new vehicle, view the vehicles already in

the system, update a record when something changes, and delete a vehicle when it is no longer needed.

We are not building a complete enterprise VMS, we are building the essential workflow

and making sure it works properly.

The application follows a very simple business journey.

First, an authorized staff member logs in.

When a new vehicle arrives, they register it in the system.

The vehicle then appears in the vehicle list.

If its information changes, the existing record can be updated instead of creating another one.

And when a vehicle leaves the fleet, it can be safely removed.

We will build each part of this journey together, one step at a time.

Once those features are working, we will not simply assume everything is correct.

You will verify the application at three simple levels.

First, a unit test will check one business rule by itself.

Next, integration testing will confirm that the connected parts of the application work together.

Finally, you will complete the entire user journey from login to deleting a vehicle.

And just like the build itself, these testing steps will be fully guided.

After the application works and the tests pass, you will take it through the final delivery steps.

You will push the project to GitHub, follow a simple CICD workflow, deploy the Flutter

frontend and Java backend, and run one final live check.

Your goal is not just to watch someone build an application.

You will follow the complete guided journey yourself.

From the first requirement to a working application, you can actually demonstrate.
